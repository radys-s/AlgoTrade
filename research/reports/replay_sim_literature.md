# Replay-sim literature scan (web search only; thin results)

Coverage: Qminers/arena.qminers.com and QminersOrg/qminers-algo-arena returned NOTHING indexed (Q4 = no public info). Prosperity/RTG top writeups found only partially. Most ideas below are my inference from theory + the sources, not published recipes for this arena. Verify against local backtests.

## Sources
- hftbacktest order-fill docs: https://hftbacktest.readthedocs.io/en/latest/order_fill.html
  Replay cannot change the market; queue position only advances on trades at your price (risk-averse) or probabilistically; taking liquidity fills regardless of size. Replay artefacts flatter size/taking, not queue-sitting.
- Databento queue position from L2: https://databento.com/blog/getting-queue-position-from-l2-and-order-book-data
  Cancels come mostly from the BACK of the queue; models that spread them evenly overstate fills. In our sim the cancels are scheduled and fixed (zombies), so this bias is inverted: ahead-of-us orders that cancel advance us for free.
- Moallemi & Yuan queue value: https://www.econbiz.de/Record/a-model-for-queue-position-valuation-in-a-limit-order-book/10012953025
  Value of queue position is ~ half-spread order of magnitude; has a static part and an option part (keeping the position). Front of queue is worth much more than back.
- Huang-Lehalle-Rosenbaum queue-reactive: https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.0563
  Arrival/cancel intensities depend on queue size. Our tape is generated by it, so queue size at touch predicts near-term depletion/refill and spread changes (signal for when to size up / step away). Reactive to OUR orders: no.
- Guéant-Lehalle-Fernandez-Tapia: https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.3115
  Inventory-bounded MM reduces to linear ODEs; optimal quotes skew with inventory, quote size is not the lever, quote depth is. Asymptotically skew is ~linear in q.
- Cartea et al. / fill-prob vs post-fill return: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.18625
  Fill probability is negatively correlated with post-fill return: easy fills are adverse-selected. In our non-reactive tape adverse selection only exists via the tape's own price process (check markout per fill).
- Chavez-Casillas et al. adaptive placement w/ partial fills: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.03086 (listed under related results; not read in detail)
- IMC Prosperity writeups (Frankfurt Hedgehogs P3 2nd: https://github.com/HendrikMS/imc-prosperity-3 ; https://kinshu0.github.io/jekyll/update/2025/07/06/prosperity-3.html)
  Standard top recipe: take mispriced -> clear inventory at zero edge -> make by pennying/overbidding the best level, with inventory skew. Reported gains over plain maker come from the "clear at fair value" step (frees capacity) and from taking. Their books REACT to pennying (bots), so pennying is NOT applicable here.
- Optiver RTG: only starter code/Avellaneda-Stoikov repos found; no winner writeup. Not useful.

## Ranked ideas for OUR rules (EV ordering, all hypotheses)
1. Capacity recycling (highest). With +-100 cap and ~400+ lots/s flow per side, PnL ~ (lots filled) x edge; lots filled is bounded by how fast inventory is flattened. Test: on each fill that pushes |inv| past ~50-60, immediately shrink/cancel the same-side quote (modify-shrink keeps priority) and size the opposite side at full, so you are always at max one-sided capacity on the side that reduces inventory. GLFT says skew is the lever, not spread. Expected: more lots/s at same 0.26 $/lot. You already do "ML shrink"; test an explicit q-dependent size schedule per side rather than binary.
2. Value-of-capacity audit: compute marginal PnL per unit of |inv| (shadow price) from your own logs; if the opposite-side fill rate when long is ~ the same-side rate, optimum is to quote full size on the reducing side and ~0 on the adding side only beyond a threshold. Moallemi-Yuan option value argues for KEEPING front-queue position on the reducing side, i.e. never cancel it, only shrink.
3. Zombie / steal exploitation at the touch: since historical orders ahead of us are not removed when we steal, a fill stolen is free extra volume. Maximise number of price-time-eligible stubs at the touch refreshed after each steal (parked stubs, which you have). Check whether stubs on the second level (1 tick behind) get stolen fills when the touch moves through - leaders may hold shallow (level 2-3) small clips that you reported as losing; re-test with per-level markout rather than total PnL.
4. Queue-reactive state gating: use touch queue size/imbalance (HLR intensities) to predict short-term depletion; size up before the touch queue on the far side is predicted to be eaten, step size down when spread widens (7 % of the time spread >1 tick: quoting there could be the leaders' extra edge: capture 2+ tick spread with full size).
5. Spread>1 regime: when spread is 2+ ticks quote at the touch (not inside; inside lost in your tests) with max size: edge per lot doubles in those 7 % of time.
6. Hold-through penalty trade: the 10,000 $/s penalty is huge vs 0.26 $/lot; only worth exceeding the cap if the excess is < 1 ms-scale. Do NOT pursue, except check that you never sit at exactly +-100 with a pending fill (5 ms latency makes overshoot possible).

Not applicable (needs impact/reactive counterparties): pennying/overbidding to move other bots, ABIDES-style agent strategies, spoofing/layering to induce reaction, any "signal the tape reacts to our quote".

## Honest gap
No public info on this arena or leaders' 650-790k construction was found. Most likely sources of the 30-50 % gap (inference): higher lots/s via faster inventory recycling (ideas 1-2), exploiting wide-spread episodes (5), and possibly a better fair-value/markout model to avoid adverse fills (signal-conditioned skew).
